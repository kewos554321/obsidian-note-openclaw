**2. 值得注意的訊號**
- **實價登錄高總價帶**：115S3 出現多筆 5,700萬～2.9億成交，單價落在 27～46 萬/㎡（約 9～15 萬/坪換算區間視坪數而異），顯示蛋黃區大坪數仍有特定買方承接，但單價離散大，個案條件差異明顯。
- **租賃掛牌活躍**：信義區、內湖區捷運宅租件密集（象山、港墘、101/世貿），反映屋主「以租待售」心態，也代表短期買賣僵持。
- **重劃區供給壓力**：台中北屯 14 期、G3 商圈店面與桃園龜山 A7 周邊持續有物件釋出，店面與大坪數產品議價空間最大。

**3. 對自住者建議**
- 蛋黃區捷運宅（信義、內湖）可挑「屋主已轉租、急售意願高」的物件議價，留意貸款成數與寬限期條件。
- 重劃區（北屯 14 期、龜山 A7）適合長期自住，但避開店面與大坪數，優先選中小坪數、管理佳的社區。

**4. 對投資者建議**
- 短期價差空間有限，勿追高；若布局以「租金報酬＋長期持有」為主，捷運沿線小宅較穩。
- 高總價大坪數流動性差，除非有明確自用或資產配置需求，否則不宜進場。

**5. 風險提醒**
緊盯央行信用管制與房貸利率走向；重劃區餘屋與店面供給量大，議價前先查同社區近半年成交與待售量，避免買在供給高峰。

#### 📚 Today's Concept: 52-Week High/Low and Moving Averages

What it is: The 52-week high/low is the highest and lowest price a stock traded at over the past year, while a moving average (MA) is the average closing price over a set window (e.g., 50 or 200 days) recalculated daily, smoothing out noise. Think of the MA as a rolling mean over a time series, and the 52-week range as the min/max of that series.

Why it matters: These tell you where price sits relative to its recent history and trend direction. A stock above its rising 200-day MA is in an uptrend; a new 52-week high signals strong momentum, while a break below the 200-day MA often warns of trend reversal.

Example: Suppose a stock's last 200 closes average $142, so its 200-day MA is $142. It trades at $155 today, near its 52-week high of $158 (low was $98). Price is 9% above the MA and the MA is sloping up — a healthy uptrend. If it instead fell to $135, it would be below the MA, a caution flag.

Rule of thumb: Treat the 200-day MA as your trend filter — stay constructive above it, get defensive below it, and be wary of new highs made on shrinking volume.

#### 🧩 Blind 100 — 703. Kth Largest Element in a Stream [Heap]
**連結:** https://leetcode.com/problems/kth-largest-element-in-a-stream/
> 📅 **Today's Daily Challenge:** #3644 Minimum Positive Sum Subarray  [Easy] — Tags: Array, Sliding Window, Prefix Sum — https://leetcode.com/problems/minimum-positive-sum-subarray/

## **Problem Type:** Heap / Priority Queue (Min-Heap of size k)

**Key Insight:** Maintain a min-heap of exactly the `k` largest elements seen so far. The heap's root is always the kth largest — anything smaller than the root can never be the kth largest, so we discard it.

**Approach:**
1. In `__init__`, push all `nums` into a min-heap, then pop until size ≤ `k`.
2. In `add(val)`: push `val` onto the heap.
3. If heap size exceeds `k`, pop the smallest (root).
4. Return `heap[0]` — the kth largest.

**Python3 Solution:**
```python
import heapq

class KthLargest:
    def __init__(self, k: int, nums: list[int]):
        self.k = k
        self.heap = nums
        heapq.heapify(self.heap)
        while len(self.heap) > k:
            heapq.heappop(self.heap)

    def add(self, val: int) -> int:
        heapq.heappush(self.heap, val)
        if len(self.heap) > self.k:
            heapq.heappop(self.heap)
        return self.heap[0]
```

**Complexity:** 
- Init: Time O(n log k) | Space O(k)
- `add`: Time O(log k) | Space O(1)

**Blind 100 Note:** This is the canonical "bounded heap" pattern — keep only the top-k elements to get O(log k) per operation instead of O(n log n) sorting. It teaches you to invert the intuition: use a **min-heap** to track the **largest** k elements (root = smallest of the big ones = kth largest). Practice next: **215. Kth Largest Element in an Array**, **347. Top K Frequent Elements**, **973. K Closest Points to Origin**, **295. Find Median from Data Stream** (two-heap variant).

**Contest Tips:**
- **Edge case:** `k == 1` → heap always holds just the max; `add` returns max so far.
- **Don't sort on every `add`** — that's O(n log n) per call and TLEs on large inputs.
- **`heapify` in `__init__`** is O(n), faster than `n` pushes (O(n log n)).
- **Reuse `nums` list** as the heap (avoid copying) — saves memory and a pass.
- **Common mistake:** using a max-heap (negating values) — unnecessary; a min-heap of size k is the clean solution.
- Python's `heapq` is min-heap only; for max-heap patterns negate values on push/pop.

#### 📷 Today's Concept: Gear — Tripod Selection: Weight, Head Type, Stability

**What it is:** Tripod selection balances three trade-offs—weight, head type, and stability—against how and where you shoot. The right tripod is the lightest one that still holds your A7C rock-steady in your worst-case conditions.

**Why it matters:** A shaky tripod ruins long exposures, timelapses, and locked-off cinematic shots—no stabilization rescues camera shake at the source. The wrong head makes smooth pans impossible for video.

**How to apply it:**
1. Weigh your heaviest rig (A7C + lens ≈ 1–1.5 kg); pick a tripod rated 2–3× that for headroom.
2. Match the head: ball head for stills/landscape (fast, compact); fluid head for video (smooth pans/tilts). A hybrid ball head with a panning base covers both.
3. Prioritize leg stability: carbon fiber for travel weight, aluminum for budget; avoid center columns extended in wind.
4. Test torsional rigidity—grab the head and twist; flex means blur.
5. For street/portrait, consider a lightweight travel tripod or monopod instead of a full rig.

**Sony A7C tip:** Turn off SteadyShot when tripod-mounted (Menu → Shooting → Image Stabilization → Off) to prevent sensor-drift blur on long exposures.

**Common mistake:** Buying a flimsy ultralight tripod that wobbles in breeze. Avoid it by testing stability at full height before buying—cheap lightness costs sharpness.

#### 📚 Today's Concept: Observability: Metrics, Logs, Traces

**What it is:** Observability is the ability to understand a system's internal state from its external outputs—metrics (aggregated numbers over time), logs (discrete timestamped events), and traces (request flows across services). Together they answer "is it healthy?", "what happened?", and "where did it go wrong?"

**When to use it:** Use all three in production distributed systems. When a checkout request is slow, metrics show latency spiked, traces reveal the payment service is the bottleneck, and logs explain why (e.g., a DB timeout).

**Example:**
```python
# Metrics: counter + histogram
orders_created.inc()
db_latency.observe(elapsed)

# Log: structured event
logger.info("order_created", order_id=oid, user_id=uid)

# Trace: span around a call
with tracer.start_span("charge_card"):
    charge(card)
```

**Gotcha:** Treating them as interchangeable. Metrics are cheap but lossy (no per-request detail); logs are detailed but expensive at scale; traces sample and miss outliers. A common mistake is logging everything and alerting on logs—use metrics for alerting, traces for debugging latency, and logs for root-cause detail.

**怎麼做：**
1. 開拍前用「我想讓觀眾知道＿＿」造句，限 15 字內。
2. 檢查是否具體：把「A7C 很好用」改成「A7C 讓新手單手拍出穩定 Vlog」。
3. 列出 3 個支撐點，每個對應一段畫面。
4. 剪輯時逐段自問「這有服務那句話嗎」，沒有就砍。
5. 片頭 10 秒內口頭說出或字卡點明這句話。

**新手常犯的錯：** 想塞太多訊息，一句話變成三句話。避免方式：若一句話講不完，就拆成兩支片。

**延伸 idea：** 拍「我用 A7C 拍一週 Vlog，只帶一顆鏡頭」——核心訊息：「一顆 35mm 就夠拍出有溫度的日常」，全片圍繞這個主張取捨素材。
